# Ethics and equity notes
- **Consent and notice.** Occupancy sensing in workplaces and homes requires informed notice and a lawful basis; this work uses PUBLIC CASAS recordings (CC BY 4.0) and no new data collection. A real deployment needs posted notice, opt-out for individuals, and purpose limitation (comfort/energy only, not performance monitoring).
- **What the privacy guarantees do NOT cover.** (i) DP-SGD guarantees are day-level for each client's released updates, NOT client-level; with ~10 clients client-level DP is not claimed. (ii) A day's raw data also appears in the 48 h input context of the next two records, so the effective protection for one day's raw data is weaker (group-privacy factor up to 3). (iii) The APF is event-level epsilon_act-DP for the presence bit at one step given the state and forecast; it does not protect a whole-day trajectory against an adversary with side information, nor leakage through the physical state or the electricity meter. (iv) MIA/actuation attacks are empirical audits, not proofs.
- **Discriminatory defaults.** Comfort defaults trained on survey data skew toward the surveyed populations (ASHRAE DB II is dominated by office buildings in specific regions; thermal-preference models can systematically favour majority thermal norms by gender, age or clothing). Personal offsets and RR feedback are meant to reduce, not remove, this.
- **Accessibility.** Some occupants need higher illuminance or stable (non-dimming, non-flickering) lighting; the 300 lx task floor and fixed >=1 kHz PWM are minimal safeguards, and a real system needs per-user override. Our synthetic lighting preferences are NOT clinical or accessibility data.
- **Legal context.** India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 is relevant to occupancy/behaviour data in monitored spaces; the statute name and scope must be verified against the official text before citing (not verified here).
- **Limits.** All preference ground truth (lighting, personas, CASAS->ASHRAE link) is synthetic; no field deployment.
